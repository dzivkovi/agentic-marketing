# Haines x Flanagan x the Anatomy: alignment, outliers, and what to change (2026-07-21)

Purpose: compare the marketing worldviews of Corey Haines (Swipe Files / Conversion Factory / the marketingskills pack) and Kieran Flanagan (HubSpot, Marketing Against the Grain, co-author of the upcoming "Loop") as expressed in seven mid-2026 talks, then test the Marketing Anatomy against their union. The output is a keep / copy / adjust / watch verdict per area, with contradictions rendered as scales (different use cases demand different points on the scale), not as right-vs-wrong.

Method note: each author's positions were distilled from full transcripts with timestamped evidence. Flanagan's MATG episodes are co-hosted; positions belonging to Kipp Bodnar (HubSpot CMO, e.g. the nine refounding principles, the six-week sprint rollout, "AI without outcomes is not marketing, it's art") are flagged as Kipp's; Kieran endorses them but they are not his original claims.

Sources (all public YouTube):
- Haines: TripleDart Coach talk https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FaiQD5_q21c (2026-06), Gerrid Smith interview https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yjj1RedLMmc (2026-06), Andrew Warner interview https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YajqB9RDdzI (2026-02)
- Flanagan: Bulldozer "one person marketing team" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pJtOBchK-0o (2026-05, richest solo source), MATG Q3 strategy https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0XlijhduLko (2026-06), MATG AI employee https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bpM9D1kQaAs (2026-07), MATG second brain https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTiMbqFwv4c (2026-07)

## 1. The two worldviews in one paragraph each

**Haines is the practitioner-craftsman, bottom-up.** Marketing decomposes into agent-executable SOPs ("a skill is a hard-coded prompt that is an SOP", yjj1RedLMmc at 03:34). The human stops being the middleman between AI and tools and becomes the orchestrator ("I'm now the orchestrator and the overseer", FaiQD5_q21c at 26:49). Execution is commoditized; the human frontier is taste and discovering NEW playbooks before they get encoded ("we can build new playbooks", FaiQD5_q21c at 52:36). The native medium is custom code at bare metal, terminal-first; SaaS abstraction is friction. Compression is enormous ("years and years of marketing work can be done in a single week by one person", YajqB9RDdzI at 00:00) and the system compounds by refining skill files after every run.

**Flanagan is the org-strategist, top-down.** Value is taste x domain expertise x AI agency, and any one without the others produces slop ("the most dangerous employee ... is someone with a small amount of domain expertise and a high amount of AI agency", pJtOBchK-0o at 00:00). AI absorbs the marketing middle tier; orgs flatten into generalists-with-agents and the handoff culture dies. Because the known playbook is commoditized inside the LLM, the only durable edge is learning velocity and deliberately operating outside best practice (0XlijhduLko at 09:02). The durable asset is compounding context: personal, team, and company second brains ("a version of yourself that never forgets and gets smarter over time", nTiMbqFwv4c at 07:49).

They are describing the same revolution from two altitudes: Haines from inside the terminal, Flanagan from inside the org chart.

## 2. The Venn

### 2.1 Shared core (both authors, independently)

1. **Skills are encoded domain expertise, not prompts.** Haines: SOP-as-executable-plan. Flanagan: "those skills are really based upon your domain expertise" (pJtOBchK-0o at 53:37). Same unit of work, arrived at from opposite directions.
2. **The human becomes an orchestrator / manager of agents.** Haines: middleman to overseer. Flanagan: "everyone is a manager" (bpM9D1kQaAs at 33:43).
3. **Execution is commoditized; taste and judgment are the residual human value.** Haines: anti-slop taste, playbook discovery. Flanagan: taste x expertise x agency.
4. **Context is the keystone.** Haines: the product-marketing context skill every other skill reads first (hub-and-spoke, yjj1RedLMmc at 13:57). Flanagan: profiles, style cards, second brains. Identical insight, different container.
5. **The system must compound.** Haines: refine the skill file after each run ("learning by osmosis"). Flanagan: mandatory feedback loops ("if you're building real systems it has to have feedback loops", pJtOBchK-0o) plus compounding brains.
6. **One-person leverage is real; teams flatten.** Haines: solo operator running 10 parallel agents. Flanagan: the one-person marketing team, death of the middle tier.
7. **Claude Code / terminal-adjacent harness is the current power tool.** Both operating models are Claude-shaped in 2026.
8. **Show, don't tell.** Flanagan says it explicitly (prototypes over memos); Haines lives it (every claim is a live demo). This is also Anatomy Commandment 2 (catalyst, not blank page) stated by two independent authorities.

### 2.2 Haines-only outliers

- **Bare metal doctrine.** Custom code beneath the SaaS layer is where agents "have full control"; no-code tools will be forced to embed agent engines; their only moat is fear of the terminal (FaiQD5_q21c at 56:10-57:39).
- **Acquisition-channel depth.** Programmatic SEO (12 playbooks), SEO audit, site architecture, schema: the deepest concrete channel playbooks either author shows.
- **Skill mechanics.** Skill stacking/chaining, horizontal-vs-vertical taxonomy, model-per-task toolmastery, GitHub as marketing infrastructure.
- **The agency reckoning.** Commodity marketing services (meta descriptions, audits) collapse in price ("there's going to be a reckoning", YajqB9RDdzI at 21:16).
- **Commercial tell:** his Magister bet (hosted agent with skills pre-installed) concedes that the mass market wants the leverage WITHOUT the terminal. His own product contradicts his bare-metal evangelism, in the direction of the Anatomy's tiering.

### 2.3 Flanagan-only outliers

- **Learning velocity as the only durable strategy.** The LLM gives everyone the same best practice, so reward migrates to those who iterate outside it (0XlijhduLko at 09:02). The thesis of the "Loop" book (joint with Kipp).
- **Org redesign.** Marketing becomes an engineer-led discipline: sprints, fluid outcome-scoped squads, hiring AI builders over traditional marketers (the implementation is Kipp's memo; the engineer-led framing is Kieran's own, 0XlijhduLko at 17:15).
- **Adoption metrics honesty.** AI usage rate is a vanity metric ("they could be using AI and doing the most dumbest shit with it", 0XlijhduLko at 07:29); marketing lacks engineering's clean productivity correlate, so leaders must be AI-native to judge.
- **The messy middle diagnosis.** 80% of the industry uses AI for everything with no clear outcomes (0XlijhduLko at 04:07).
- **AI mascots.** Most brands will front a disclosed synthetic avatar with a human content team as its brains (pJtOBchK-0o at 18:30). His most contrarian bet.
- **Channel shift.** Branded channels decay, personality-led channels win; B2C leads B2B by 3-5 years.
- **Autonomous marketing tools are terrible** because AI is not a replacement for domain expertise (0XlijhduLko at 05:40). A direct market-shape claim.

### 2.4 Shared blind spots (the space both vacate)

These are the areas neither author covers, inferred from absence across all seven talks:

1. **Governance.** Haines demos in "dangerously skip permissions ... YOLO mode"; Flanagan's second brains ingest email, Slack, and docs with zero discussion of access control, compliance, or what happens when the employee leaves with the brain. Neither has HITL gates, write-permission models, audit trails, or observability.
2. **Outcome measurement rigor.** Haines's evidence is throughput (pages, hours), never revenue lift. Flanagan concedes measurement is unsolved and falls back to leader intuition. Neither has an attribution posture.
3. **Regulated-enterprise reality.** Legal review, brand stewardship, procurement, data privacy and consent when agents hold CRM and email access: absent in both.
4. **Vendor concentration.** Both operating models are Claude-mono with no portability hedge.
5. **The transition path for the 10-500 person org.** Haines speaks from solo/agency context, Flanagan from HubSpot-scale enablement; the mid-size org in between gets only cultural advice.
6. **Demand-side GEO.** How buyers now discover via AI answers is nearly absent from both (Flanagan shows a dashboard b-roll; Haines has an ai-seo skill but frames it production-side).

This intersection-of-absence is precisely the Anatomy's occupied territory: Commandments 4-7, the NFR appendix, the size tiers, and the GEO multi-verb rows. The competitive read: the two most-followed voices in agentic marketing are training the market to need exactly the layer this framework provides.

## 3. What marketing technologists and marketers should watch in H2 2026 (reprioritized)

Ranked synthesis of the union of both authors plus this framework's own additions (items 6-8):

1. **Encode your expertise into skills NOW, while practiced marketers still have the edge.** Both authors agree this is the window: AI rewards those who did real marketing for years and can write it down. The mediocre tier is absorbed first.
2. **Build the keystone context asset before any skill.** One context layer (product marketing context / second brain) that every workflow reads. Without it, every skill produces generic output; with it, everything compounds.
3. **Wire feedback loops into every system.** A skill that is not refined after each run is a static prompt; the compounding is the point. This is the practical, human-curated version of LEARN, distinct from unsupervised self-mutation.
4. **Reprice what you buy.** Commodity execution (audits, meta descriptions, templated pages, first-draft copy) is collapsing in price. Audit every agency retainer and SaaS subscription against what a skill does in minutes. Expect the vendors to embed agent engines and reprice ahead of you.
5. **Redesign work around outcomes, not handoffs.** Sprints, generalists-with-agents, prototype-first communication. Measure outcomes of AI work, never usage rates (usage is a vanity metric on both authors' evidence).
6. **Impose the governance layer yourself, because the creator ecosystem will not.** HITL on anything state-changing, default-deny writes, day-1 observability. The YOLO demos work on regenerable local artifacts; they do not survive contact with a brand, a regulator, or a customer database. Adopt the leverage, keep the gates.
7. **Hedge the harness.** Both authors are Claude-shaped today; skills as portable text plus MCP as the connector is the insurance policy that survives a model-market shift (Haines himself predicts harness convergence).
8. **Watch two demand-side shifts the operators underweight:** GEO (buyers researching in AI answers; EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure obligations land August 2026 and touch Flanagan's mascot bet directly) and the personality-led channel migration (branded-channel decay).

## 4. Gap analysis against the Marketing Anatomy

### 4.1 KEEP (validated, sometimes verbatim, by both authors)

- **Commandment 2 (catalyst, not blank page).** Flanagan's "show don't tell" and Haines's fork-then-customize workflow are the same principle from two independent authorities. Keep with confidence.
- **Commandment 3 (co-create; business owns the logic in plain text).** Skills-as-SOPs written by domain experts IS this commandment operationalized. Haines's origin story (agency SOPs fed to Claude, 22 skills in 30 minutes) is the existence proof.
- **Commandments 5-7 (augment + HITL, default-deny writes, day-1 observability).** Not contradicted; UNaddressed by both authors, which makes them the uncontested moat (section 2.4). Keep and lead with them in enterprise contexts.
- **Commandment 4 (decouple logic from platform).** Both authors' Claude-mono posture is the risk this commandment insures against. Haines's harness-convergence prediction supports it.
- **The 8-verb decomposition and scored inventory.** Haines's 40-skill library maps cleanly onto the verbs (the Awesome list already proves it row by row). Neither author has a map of the whole territory; both are anecdote-first. The inventory remains the differentiating artifact.
- **The 3 size tiers.** They resolve the loudest apparent contradiction in the field (see scale S1 below) and neither author has an equivalent.
- **GEO as a multi-verb category.** Both authors underweight the demand side; the Anatomy's SENSE/SHIP/MEASURE GEO rows are ahead of both.
- **The direct-response lineage.** Kipp's "AI without outcomes is not marketing, it's art" is Dan Kennedy's results-rule ethos restated for 2026. The framework's Kennedy/Jackson/Abraham roots are not legacy baggage; they are the taste-and-outcomes layer both authors say survives commoditization. Keep the lineage visible in teaching.

### 4.2 COPY (their strengths, your gaps; adopt with attribution)

1. **The keystone context skill pattern (Haines).** Make "context skill first, all other skills read it" an explicit architecture rule in skills/README.md, and consider promoting it to skill #0 ahead of the fifteen T1 candidates. The Anatomy has audience profiling (KNOW, 70) but not the hub-and-spoke read-order convention. Cheap to adopt, immediately improves every downstream skill.
2. **The taste x domain expertise x AI agency triad (Flanagan).** Adopt as teaching language for readiness assessment; "the dangerous employee" (high agency, low expertise) is the sharpest one-line diagnostic of slop risk available and lands instantly in workshops.
3. **Skill refinement loop as an explicit LEARN activity (both).** Add a scored row: post-run skill/SOP refinement (human-curated, per-run). Distinct from the existing Self-Optimizing Feedback Loop row (T3, autonomous self-mutation, rightly gated). Suggested scoring: high universality, high leverage; it is arguably the single highest-ROI habit in the whole agentic stack and currently invisible in the inventory.
4. **Learning velocity as a strategy-layer principle (Flanagan).** The commoditized-playbook argument deserves a place in DECIDE guidance: when the LLM gives everyone the same best practice, differentiated strategy comes from iterating outside it. Candidate phrasing for an eighth commandment if it proves durable in practice; hold it as a DECIDE note until then. Self-referential caution: the Anatomy itself is a best-practice document; its edge is the scored specificity and governance layer, not the generic advice, and this principle explains why.
5. **Adoption-outcome instrumentation (Flanagan/Kipp).** Add to MEASURE guidance: measure outcomes of AI-assisted work; never report usage rates as success. One sentence, prevents the most common enterprise adoption theater.
6. **Skill stacking and model-per-task notes (Haines).** Fold into the skills/README anatomy table as design considerations (chained skills passing outputs; tool-calling models vs writing models).

### 4.3 ADJUST (re-weight, do not rewrite)

- **LEARN verb is underweighted relative to the field's center of gravity.** Both authors put compounding at the CENTER of their systems; the Anatomy treats LEARN as a closing verb with two rows. The fix is the new refinement-loop row (4.2.3) plus one framing sentence in the verb description: LEARN is where agentic systems earn their compounding, not an afterthought.
- **KNOW verb: consider "compounding context assets" framing.** Flanagan's personal-to-team-to-company brain progression maps exactly onto the Solo/SMB/Enterprise tiers and is a better generalization of what KNOW infrastructure means in the agentic era than audience intelligence alone. The decoupled-RAG NFR section already contains the enterprise half; connect the two.
- **Org-design guidance is a live gap for consulting (not necessarily for the repo).** Sprints, fluid squads, AI-builder hiring, the fate of the middle tier: the Anatomy's co-creation model covers the IT-business lanes but not marketing-org redesign. Decide deliberately whether this stays out of repo scope (it is Kipp's published territory and the Loop book will claim it) and lives in engagement material instead.

### 4.4 WATCH (do not adopt yet; monitor with named triggers)

- **AI mascots / synthetic brand faces (Flanagan).** Provocative, evidenced (avatar accounts growing fastest), and colliding head-on with EU AI Act Article 50 disclosure obligations (August 2026) that the compliance lens already tracks. Watch trigger: a major B2B brand fronting a disclosed avatar without backlash. If it lands, it enters MAKE as a scored row with a heavy compliance note.
- **The agency reckoning (Haines).** Real for commodity deliverables; watch trigger for the Anatomy's sourcing cheat sheet: first evidence of agencies repricing audits/retainers structurally (not anecdotally).
- **Autonomous marketing tools failing (Flanagan).** Supports the tool-review procedure's skepticism; keep using the 3-state verdict rather than adopting his blanket dismissal (some paid tools already clear his own bar).

### 4.5 REMOVE

Nothing. The exercise strengthened rather than weakened every existing commandment. The one candidate examined for removal (the T3 gating of the Self-Optimizing Feedback Loop, which both authors seem to contradict by centering compounding) survives on closer read: what they center is human-curated refinement, which is the NEW row, not autonomous self-mutation, which stays gated. The distinction is the framework's contribution, not its error.

## 5. Contradictions as scales (not verdicts)

**S1. Bare metal vs buy incumbent.** Haines: custom code, bypass SaaS. The Anatomy: enterprise buys incumbent and rides MCP on top. Resolution: this is the Solo-to-Enterprise axis, not a disagreement. Haines's advice is the Solo tier's DIY posture taken to its logical extreme, and it breaks exactly where marketing stops owning the codebase. His own hosted-product bet concedes the scale. Placement rule: how much of your stack do you control, and who audits your changes?

**S2. YOLO autonomy vs HITL mandate.** Haines: let her rip overnight. The Anatomy: drafts only for state change. Resolution: risk-tier the artifact, not the workflow. Regenerable local artifacts (drafts, pages in a branch, research) tolerate full autonomy; state-changing actions (publish, send, spend) get gates. Flanagan already operates mid-scale ("email the first thousand and then we'll review"). Placement rule: can the action be reverted for free?

**S3. Personal idiosyncratic systems vs standardized SOPs.** Flanagan: "the system is very much built to how I work ... I can just vibe." The Anatomy: co-created, governed, shared skills. Resolution: a maturity scale. Personal vibe systems are where taste gets encoded (the R&D lab); team standardization is how the best workflow scales to everyone (Flanagan himself: "learn from the best and then scale those workflows to the rest"). Placement rule: is this workflow still being discovered, or is it being operated?

**S4. Move-fast adoption vs governance-first adoption.** Both authors: adopt now, the window rewards speed. The Anatomy: gates first. Resolution: parallel lanes, not sequence. Prototype at creator speed in the sandbox tier (Commandment 2 says arrive with working samples); govern before production (NFR appendix). The Double Diamond already encodes this: diverge fast, converge safely. Placement rule: sandbox or production?

## 6. Positioning read (for the author's practice)

The three worldviews form a stable three-legged stool:

- **Haines owns the craft layer:** skills, stacking, code-native execution. Solo-to-SMB center of gravity.
- **Flanagan owns the strategy-and-org layer:** learning velocity, org flattening, compounding context. Scale-up-to-enterprise-culture center of gravity.
- **This framework owns the adoption-architecture layer:** the map (verbs + scored inventory + size tiers), the governance (commandments + NFRs), and the portability (skills + MCP, vendor-agnostic). Regulated-enterprise center of gravity.

The uncontested space, confirmed twice now (the curation log's frontier-gap finding, and section 2.4 above): governed, portable, enterprise-grade agentic marketing with strategy-layer skills. Both creators train the market toward the need; neither serves it.

Two full-circle notes worth using in teaching: the author's 2009 Inbound Marketing certification was signed by HubSpot's CEO, and in 2026 HubSpot's own SVP supplies the org-layer half of this synthesis; and the direct-response lineage (Kennedy's results-rule) turns out to be the exact taste-and-outcomes residual both authors say survives AI commoditization. The decades of practice are not the legacy to defend; they are the domain-expertise input the triad says is scarce.
