# Six-month revalidation of the Marketing Anatomy (2026-11-04)

Dated evidence behind [CHANGELOG v2.3](../CHANGELOG.md). The method is [../MARKETING-ANATOMY.md](../MARKETING-ANATOMY.md); this file is the receipts. It is correct as of 2026-11-04 and is not maintained.

## Method

Seven passes, all run on 2026-11-04:

- **Three evidence sweeps over a corpus of practitioner talks and tutorials** (public YouTube, transcribed with timestamps), one per verb group, restricted to material published 2026-05-01 or later. About two hundred searches in total; at least two per scored row. Every quote below is verbatim and carries a timestamped link.
- **Two attribution checks** against the creators the Anatomy credits: Chase AI (his full 2026 output) and Rick Mulready (three videos transcribed for the purpose, since none had been read before).
- **A web fact-check** of nineteen external claims (standards, vendors, platform names, forecasts, titles).
- **An independent second-model review**: the Anatomy, the July 2026 gap analysis and two fresh creator briefings were given to a different model with no stake in the result and six questions.
- **Two creator briefings** built from the authors' own newsletters (23 issues from Kieran Flanagan, 10 from Grace Leung, May to November 2026) and 26 podcast episodes.

Bias to keep in mind: the corpus over-represents AI builders and creators who sell AI workflows. Enterprise functions (compliance, localization, PR, events, churn) came back thin, which means people in this corpus do not talk about them, not that the scores are wrong. One cited creator's GEO video is a declared vendor partnership, and the podcast hosts quoted most often are executives of a company that sells an AI-visibility tracker.

## What held

- **The eight verbs.** Nobody in six months of practitioner talk works in a way the verbs cannot describe. Both reviews reached for new rows and reworded rules, not new verbs.
- **The size tiers, the Double Diamond, the co-creation model, the framework mapping.** Unchallenged.
- **Default-deny writes (5.2) and Day-1 observability (5.5).** Confirmed by teams running agents in production, with additions (below).
- **Repurposing as the safest large win.** The most-built agent pattern of the period: "it runs this content cascade skill where it then fetches the transcript from that video and then it rewrites a blog, a LinkedIn post and a tweet" (Chase AI, 2026-07-15, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gUv7VqcRzok&t=881). Caution from another creator: "I got shadowbanned because I was just, like, spamming through an API" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yQkzwon5Kh8&t=427).
- **Judgment stays human.** "AI is better than an average marketer, and a great marketer knows how to take this and turn it into something" (Kieran Flanagan, 2026-08-27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PFKQKZ3YB8&t=677). "You never outsource your thinking to AI" (2026-10-01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSBLYJeawC0&t=607).

## The copy-drafting question

The April score of 85 described capability. Capability is real: a ghostwriter agent whose draft is "quite like how I speak, especially the way I transition between sections" (Grace Leung, 2026-10-16, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JWdvTUZoN0s&t=1092), and voice-file systems whose output is "by far, the best first draft that I have found" (Barbara Jovanovic, 2026-08-20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_KJOJdA___k&t=2489).

Adoption among the practitioners with the largest audiences moved the other way across the period. July: "I do not use my system to create finished pieces of content. I do not copy and paste from AI" (Kieran Flanagan, 2026-07-30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cSz_6SNEirU&t=468). October: "when it comes to writing, that we're all going completely away from AI" (Kipp Bodnar, 2026-10-27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D6ejATXER0Q&t=76); "I try not to use it at all" (Kieran Flanagan, same episode, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D6ejATXER0Q&t=140); "the best way to grow an audience and to create great content is to use no AI at all" (2026-10-29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1_Lh95jRWDY&t=368). His newsletter of 2026-10-30 states the policy: automate none of the actual writing; keep AI as a structural editor and a monthly performance analyst.

The middle position most teams converge on is an explicit policy: "We don't publish anything front facing that is just written by AI. We use AI constantly, we use AI for everything" (Lon Harris, LAUNCH, 2026-08-29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YmAeVmS-bF8&t=5030). The reason given is reader trust, not model quality: readers "they'll just trust you less" (Greg Isenberg, 2026-09-02, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9_SZFIW7tus&t=217); "even as models get better ... slop doesn't seem to go away. It just keeps on scaling" (Louis-Francois Bouchard, 2026-10-22, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_dTF3NpPzGo&t=57).

Decision: split the row. Drafts and variants from approved inputs keep 80; publishable copy under a named voice gets 55, with AI's leverage there moved to research, structure, editing and analysis. The second model proposed a flat provisional 50; the split keeps the demonstrated capability visible and names where it stops paying.

## Commandments: the evidence for the rewording

**4, portability.** "They do not share memory, even if you're pointing them at the same folder" (Grace Leung, newsletter 2026-05-14); her October migration translates CLAUDE.md to AGENTS.md and converts sub-agents from Markdown to TOML (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cAeKDWa77xI&t=546). The standard itself won: Agent Skills was donated to the Agentic AI Foundation on 2026-10-22 with more than seventy adopting platforms (https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation).

**5, approval on every state change.** Contradicted at volume: "we've started just kind of rubber stamping these" (Regan Bell, YC, 2026-09-07, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n9xKblqyQ28&t=3255); "once you're operating agents in parallel or in production, this becomes completely onerous" (John Feminella, Two Sigma, 2026-10-27, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iniryju23Sk&t=961); "forcing a human in the loop approval just kind of passes the access control onto a consent-fatigued user" (Kim Maida, Keycard, 2026-10-12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ws9XcGGPBDk&t=348). Held for sends and money: "Sending is an action gate. Nothing leaves the firm until a person approves that exact email" (Karthic Rao, 2026-10-12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g0g1ncwzG0&t=612). The compaction failure: an agent "compacted its context and lost the part that said, 'Wait for my approval,' and then it deleted hundreds of emails" (Two Sigma, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iniryju23Sk&t=961).

**7, native tooling as the test.** "prompt injection itself isn't solved, and we cannot really solve it. All we can do is just to limit the blast radius" (Moritz Johner, Form3, 2026-07-20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LqLoYksJ6do&t=720); "Since they're non-deterministic, it means that it's really hard to put hardcoded guardrails" (Mistral, 2026-09-24, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CGq9KRSb9Kc&t=3541). Additions practitioners run on top of any platform: an enforcement layer that treats "the agent software as a black box" (Deno, 2026-08-17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MkRYPFIMCSA&t=835); sandboxes with "limited blast radius" (Docker, 2026-08-20, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zaGyGgLW3SM&t=331).

**Confirmed, with additions.** 5.2: "We mostly keep this to be read-only in the database, but we do allow for writes via human-reviewed bulk upserts" (YC, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n9xKblqyQ28&t=3255); "Give every agent you run its own identity instead of handing it your credentials" (Nate B. Jones, 2026-08-17, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4f5AJrJPilM&t=880); "scoped and short-lived credentials for everything that we do" (Keycard, 2026-10-28, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58ulJViGFgU&t=501); "separating the fields that agents are updating from the fields that deterministic systems are updating" (Clay, 2026-08-26, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UhCY231d0FQ&t=716). 5.5: "every agentic action attributable to a human being, should be logged somewhere outside of the agent's own reach" (Coder, 2026-10-14, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wb2v9Phhnns&t=1695); "agent systems drift constantly. Model changes, you have a new version every couple of weeks" (Meta, 2026-06-25, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vljxQZfJ9wY&t=279); memory consolidation as a platform feature (Anthropic, 2026-05-23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=geUv4CjPpxI&t=1393); memory poisoning (Bloomberg, 2026-10-23, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5O6jLtoeKrg&t=628); "a small subset should end up becoming what changes your agent's memory" (LangChain, 2026-10-08, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KGFyOtl5ktI&t=768).

**Operating limits (new 5.6).** "max turns, max parallelism, max spend" (TikTok, 2026-08-29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hD9-V56FNRI&t=853); "a token budget cap, and we also put a 15-minute wall-clock time" (Arize, 2026-08-12, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_wep53jsDD8&t=431); a two-dollar research cap per task (Kieran Flanagan, 2026-10-13, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RAuGCWZjYfo&t=682); compensation actions (TikTok, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hD9-V56FNRI&t=761); "never let the writer approve the work" (Cole Medin, 2026-09-01, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbylWXukvR8&t=828); the five audit questions (OpenAI, 2026-07-29, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BInpv7lGp1o&t=881); allowlist, blocklist and probation list for agent buyers (SoFi, 2026-10-30, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YU1uD_YkpjU&t=649).
